Bounded visual memory: every result so far, by environment

The model sees the last 4 frames plus 2 retained history frames. Two things have to hold: the predictor must benefit when the right history frames are kept (the oracle check), and a learned scorer must find those frames online without seeing the future (the method).

Earlier fresh-seed confirmation of the linear version, 23 Oct

96 new scene pairs, everything frozen beforehand. The deletion-cost signal was fitted with a masked ridge probe, not the current network. The preregistered criterion failed: it did not beat its shuffled-label control, and reader attention and diversity were better.

MSE difference, masked cost minus control (negative favours cost): shuffled cost −0.0003 [−0.0036, +0.0036]; reader attention +0.0089 [+0.0045, +0.0134]; diversity +0.0078 [+0.0029, +0.0129].

Synergy, decoy and redundancy

✓Synergy confirmed on a locked seed

Three cue cards arrive in a row and one must be deleted. This tests whether the scorer has to look at the set, not at single frames. In synergy two cards with matching tags must both be kept, because the answer is the XOR of their payloads. In decoy one eye-catching card is irrelevant. In redundancy two cards repeat the same direction, so one of them is the right one to drop.

Synergy task, locked confirmation, 26 Oct

Seed 20261104, 48 families, 2,304 decisions. The metric is how often the deleted card was one the answer needed, so lower is better. Two separately trained readers; students are means of 3 seeds.

  • All three preregistered claims passed. Ours beats full-set sensitivity by 31 and 37 points, with family-bootstrap intervals well clear of zero.
  • The same teacher without set conditioning (independent student) loses the evidence about 62% of the time, which is the argument for scoring the set jointly.
  • The MSE oracle picks the deletion with the lowest error over a 32-plan bank. It still drops needed evidence 10 to 23% of the time, so lowest feature MSE and keeping the right evidence are not the same target here.

All three tasks, both readers

Necessary-evidence loss rate, %. Uniform and visual-diversity baselines were not computed on this confirmation set.

  • Redundancy: ours loses 33 to 34%, the same as FIFO. The student does not generalize on this task.
  • Decoy: ours loses about 4%, but an appearance-only control gets 0%, so the task can be solved by looks alone.
  • Each reader has a single training seed.

Motion v2

✕Negative, closed 9 Nov

A ball drifts in the upper lane, is kicked into the lower lane, and is then hidden behind a shutter. Its direction after the kick can only be read from two visible frames of the new segment. The two siblings end the new segment at the same position with opposite velocities, so one frame is never enough.

Oracle check: can the predictor use the right two frames?

96 validation pairs, mean of 3 predictor seeds, horizon +3. Position MSE is in normalized coordinates, decoded from predicted features. Lower is better.

Yes. The correct pair is the only condition that beats the direction-blind floor, the best error a predictor can reach when it gives both siblings the same answer. All six preregistered contrasts passed.

Online retention policies

Same 96 validation pairs, ordinary gap, horizon +3. Success means both memory slots hold new-segment frames at the query.

  • All 48 deletion-cost and shuffled-label students selected step 0 on validation loss. The 5.9% shown for ours is an untrained network, not a trained method.
  • Plain attention already reaches 100%, so even a trained student would have nothing to add here.
  • Post-hoc analysis: about two thirds of labelled decisions contain no new-segment frame at all, so the teacher signal is mostly uninformative.
  • The post-hoc best pair reaches the lowest position error while holding the two cue frames only 53% of the time. As in synergy, lowest error and the intended evidence are different targets.

Motion v1 in the Phase 1 gate, 20 Oct

147 of 155 checks passed. The 8 failures are all long-gap deletion against reject-newest: choosing by predictor cost lowered feature MSE by 9.4 to 11.1%, but every paired interval for decoded position included zero.

PushT with a hidden centre of gravity

…Pending: no retention result yet

The T block looks the same under three centre-of-gravity classes, and turns differently when pushed. In the current version (v5) a red dot marks the centre of gravity for 3 early frames. The block is untouched until the test push after the query, so everything between the marker and the push is pixel-identical across classes.

What exists so far: can I-JEPA features reveal the class?

MLP probe accuracy on validation, given the listed frames. Three classes, so chance is 0.33. A usable testbed needs the recent window at chance and cue frames well above it.

  • v1 and v2 have a readable cue but leak: v1 uses a scene cut, and in v2 the start pose alone gives the class away (0.76 from two state numbers).
  • v4 is leak-free and natural, but the cue is not readable from frozen features. The class is in the simulator state and is lost on the way through I-JEPA:

v4 validation accuracy of a CoG classifier. Decoded poses come from a per-frame regressor on I-JEPA features (median error 4.2 px, 3.1°).
